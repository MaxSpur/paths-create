# Movement fitting and GNSS pilot

The NetMob UI now defaults to **Fit duration using → Simulated movement (calibrated preset)**. Every valid endpoint/itinerary candidate receives a cheap duration-only evaluation; the closest complete route wins. The original **ORS / timetable estimates** option remains available. The low-level `FitSettings.durationModel` is optional for compatibility: omitting it preserves routing-only scoring. Manual generation is unchanged.

Select a generated route and open **Movement & GNSS pilot** to create dense observations. Its initial settings reproduce the movement duration used during fitting. Editing these preview parameters changes the preview, not the fitted endpoints or original fit. Generation never allocates dense observations or evaluates noise for discarded candidates.

## Timing contract

- `lib/movementSimulation.ts` owns deterministic kinematic phases and observations (`movement-gnss-2`). Seeds combine trip identity and the simulation seed; motion and noise use separate streams. Parameters are fixed across every candidate in a job. Survey duration is used to rank candidate endpoints, never to adjust a candidate's speeds, waits or timestamps.
- The calibrated preset uses walking 5.4 km/h; cycling 18, electric bicycle 20 and scooter 12 regardless of geometry proxy; Metro 60, RER 90, train 110, tram 40 and bus 30 km/h. Driving uses the ORS leg-average speed (`distance / duration`) times a shared factor of 1, bounded to 10–130 km/h. This differentiates faster and slower routes without claiming road-specific speed limits. Missing/invalid ORS timing falls back to 40 km/h. These are modeled assumptions, not measured traffic.
- Acceleration/braking are symmetric and mode-dependent, 0.7–1.2 m/s² (walking 0.8). Short sections use triangular profiles; longer ones accelerate, cruise and brake. Every leg/stop starts and ends at rest. Curvature, gradients, queues, actual intersections and signal phases are not modeled.
- Boarding waits retain the existing timetable estimate. Intermediate transit stations receive 25 seconds dwell; the final station gets no extra dwell. `netmobRouting.ts` attaches intermediate-stop anchors from the chosen pattern before candidate evaluation. No extra routing requests are required. Older routes lacking anchors disclose that limitation.
- The current calibrated preset adds **zero extra random road stops**. The tested positive stop densities worsened duration agreement on the development sample. This does not imply absence of real red lights: total survey duration cannot identify their number/location, and the ORS average is already an aggregate estimate. A positive preview rate still samples exponential stop spacing for driving/cycling/scooters, with waits uniformly 0.5–1.5 times the configured mean (15 seconds when enabled). These stops are synthetic positions, not verified crossings.
- `NetmobFit` distinguishes `durationModel`, `movementVersion`, `movementS`, `routingS` and the ranked `modeledS`. Original ordered-leg `durationS`, waiting and offsets remain routing/timetable estimates. The UI labels the fitted total as simulated and shows the original estimate separately. Model/version and the complete preset are retained per result and in retry snapshots.
- `movement.worker.ts` creates selected-route observations off the UI thread; cancelling terminates it. `ui/movementPanel.ts` resets outdated observations on route/settings changes and initializes from the route's frozen settings. Its red overlay is separate from the clean route and honors path opacity.

## Noise and storage

For full-run planning, the [local runner](NETMOB_RUNS.md) uses a separate compact geometric variation: bounded, seeded offsets at existing vertices. It avoids dense observations entirely and does not alter movement fitting. The selected-route observation model below remains available for explicit sampling experiments.

Smooth spatial Gaussian covariance is approximated by 32 seeded Fourier features per horizontal axis: `sigma² exp(-(distance/range)²)`. Local drift uses an exponential OU recurrence with decay `exp(-deltaDistance/range - deltaTime/timeCorrelation)`, so it can evolve while stationary. White Gaussian error is independent per observation. Defaults are sigma/range 0.5 m/100 m smooth, 5 m/20 m local and 1 m white, with 30 seconds time correlation. Sigma is per axis; range is an e-folding scale. This approximates the supplied slide and is not a validated receiver model. No vertical noise or underground reception gaps are modeled.

No automatic route/observation files, browser database or persistent observation cache. A dense preview/export retains one trajectory, capped at **50,000 points**, checked before allocation. Duration-only fitting bypasses this observation cap because it creates phases only. Cost is O(vertices + phases); dense noise adds O(observations × 32). The short-lived worker and preview are released on cancellation or selection/settings changes.

Observation export remains a separate GeoParquet Point schema: noisy `geometry`, clean `truth_geometry`, elapsed synthetic seconds, sample/leg indices, cumulative clean distance, clean speed and move/wait state. Footer `odc.observations` schema 1 retains full source linkage, movement settings/phases/version and noise assumptions. Keep the original path export for unsampled geometry. The known in-app browser Blob-download problem remains unresolved; export work was explicitly outside this calibration task.

## Calibration strategy and reproduction

Only local ORS is queried. Existing Parquet/geography/transit assets are reused. No candidate geometry or dense observations are written to disk; the harness retains scalar candidate durations for comparisons and writes small JSON reports in ignored `local/`. Bounded benchmark bundles are in `.benchmark-output/`; wrappers disable public-asset copying and replace their own bundle directories.

1. `MOVEMENT_CALIBRATION_STAGE=fixed npm run calibrate:movement`: 600 hash-random eligible rows with usable departure/duration, excluding participants in the earlier 100-row pilot when its report is present. Participant-hash partitions select 240 training, 120 validation and 240 initial test rows. Compare 135 predeclared profiles on identical candidate routes. Fit shared parameters on the original routing winners; rank by mean absolute error divided by tolerance, capped at 5 to bound outlier influence. Validation selects among the five best training profiles. Assess movement-based endpoint re-ranking separately.
2. `npm run calibrate:movement`: reuse only those 360 development rows, compare ORS-derived road cruises with fixed factors, road-stop rates, waits, walking speeds and station dwells (96 combinations plus the old baseline). Freeze the chosen settings before fresh testing. The first experiment's test set is now development evidence, not reused as an independent final result.
3. `npm run validate:movement`: requires both calibration reports and the earlier `local/movement-pilot-results.json`; excludes every previously sampled participant and draws 400 fresh hash-random eligible rows. Compare the frozen model at matched 12/24/48 trial budgets, preserving each budget's actual deterministic proposal sequence. The bounded street cache reuses requests across comparisons. Simulate a dense noisy preview on each successful 12-trial winner and verify exact agreement with its fit duration. No parameter selection uses these fresh results.

The earlier pilot report can be supplied as `local/movement-pilot-results.json` or `.benchmark-output/movement/results.json` for calibration. Without it the calibration records zero previous-pilot exclusions, changing its sample; retain reports together for exact reproduction. Sampling seeds, exclusions, source/network versions and failures accompany the results. Equal mode counts are not imposed: rare modes therefore have small samples. These results concern eligible rows, not a weighted population estimate. Endpoint fitting against reported time makes this conditional reconstruction, not independent validation of true speeds or red-light behavior.

## Results

The initial fixed-speed experiment routed 584/600 rows in 92.8 seconds. Evaluating 135 settings on 5,948 candidates took 6.02 seconds total; routing dominated. On its initial held-out group, movement-aware selection raised acceptable simulated fits from 145/233 to 171/233 and reduced median error from 2.62 to 1.13 minutes. Constant car speed remained a major limitation.

The subsequent road-speed development experiment routed 351/360 rows in 45.2 seconds. On validation, acceptable fits rose from 76/118 with the old post-route simulator to 85/118 with the frozen model and movement-aware selection. Fresh validation on 2026-09-21 used **400 new random rows**, excluding 621 previously sampled participant identities. The preset was frozen before this run; no fresh result was used to choose parameters.

| 12 | 387 / 400 | 309 / 387 (79.8%) | 1.07 min | 13.93 min |
| 24 | 389 / 400 | 326 / 389 (83.8%) | 0.49 min | 10.24 min |

The old post-route simulator accepted 266/387 (68.7%) at 12 trials on the same candidate routes. There were 54 newly acceptable rows and 11 regressions. The original ORS/timetable fit accepted 309/387: the calibrated movement model now preserves aggregate fit coverage while producing explicit kinematics, though individual winners/results differ. At 12 trials, walking accepted 106/116, car-driver 96/123, Metro 29/35, RER/train 31/44, bicycle 18/27 and bus 11/17. Other modes have very small samples. Within-area trips accepted 145/159 (91.2%), median error 0.53 min.

Evaluating movement durations for 27,231 valid candidate events took **408 ms total** (~0.015 ms/event). Generating dense noisy observations for all 387 successful 12-trial winners took **606 ms total**; every preview reproduced its fitted duration. These are Node compute timings, excluding worker startup/rendering. Production evaluates duration once per candidate; the instrumented comparison also reevaluated it through the production path.

The complete 12/24/48 comparison took **488.7 s**, 34,855 local ORS requests and 25,537 cache hits. Its successive budget phases took 78.9 / 135.7 / 274.1 s and made 8,513 / 8,793 / 17,549 additional requests. Later phases reuse earlier street queries, so these are **not standalone cold-run cost estimates**. Keep 12 as the economical default; 24 is a useful tighter-fit option. Going from 24 to 48 added only seven acceptable rows and incurred substantially more routing work.

At 48 trials, 59 valid routes still missed tolerance: 19 observed durations were below, and 39 above, the entire sampled duration range. Eight records still lacked a sequence-compatible itinerary within access limits. These are residual sampling/routing/model limitations, not justification for stretching timestamps or tuning a row's speeds. More candidate endpoints help, but do not guarantee every survey row can be explained.

Full-process Node peak RSS was **2,826 MiB**, including import, network/timing assets and routing search heaps; it excludes ORS Java and is not a browser-memory estimate or a full-run safety claim. The fresh report is 662 KB; all three new calibration/validation reports total about 1.0 MB. No routes/observation files were written. Temporary benchmark bundles were removed after verification. Detailed scalar results: `local/movement-calibration-results.json`, `local/movement-calibration-road-results.json`, `local/movement-validation-results.json`.

Verification covers deterministic row seeds, original geometry/timing preservation, duration-only/preview equality, bounded observations, shared cruise rules independent of observed targets, station dwell, stationary noise, cancellation and propagation of fitted settings into the preview. Full checks mock public services. Spatial covariance accuracy, road-level controls and full-dataset memory behavior remain separate follow-up measurements.

Live-browser verification on 2026-09-21 confirmed the new scoring selector, EMG import, selected-row generation, and exact preview agreement: row 17 scored 22.9 simulated minutes against 20 recorded and 24.4 from routing; the dense preview reproduced 22.9 minutes with 689 observations. Saved Manual places/settings remained intact. `npm run check` passed 200 tests, build and the initial-bundle budget; calibration/validation harnesses also passed a separate TypeScript check. No export interaction was performed for this task.
